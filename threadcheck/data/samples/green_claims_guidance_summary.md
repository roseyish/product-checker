# Green Claims Guidance: Working Summary (paraphrased, not the original text)

This summary paraphrases principles common to the UK Competition and Markets Authority Green Claims Code
and EU rules on environmental marketing. It is provided as regulatory context for the verifier.

1. Truthful and accurate. A claim must be correct in what it states and in the overall impression it creates.
2. Clear and unambiguous. Generic terms such as sustainable, eco-friendly, green, conscious or natural should
   not be used without explaining exactly what they refer to and how they are substantiated.
3. No material omissions. A claim about one attribute must not hide a significant negative impact elsewhere,
   and a claim about part of a product or range must not imply it covers the whole.
4. Fair comparisons. Comparative claims must name the comparison point and use like-for-like data.
5. Full lifecycle. Claims should consider the full product lifecycle, or state the boundary clearly.
6. Substantiated. Claims must be backed by robust, credible, up-to-date evidence available before publication.
7. Carbon claims. Terms such as carbon neutral, net zero or climate positive must disclose the emissions scope,
   the baseline, and whether the outcome relies on offsets. Claims based on offsetting without an explicit
   explanation are treated as likely misleading.
8. Certifications and labels. Sustainability labels must be based on a certification scheme with independent
   verification and traceable licence or certificate references. Self-created labels without third-party
   verification should not be presented as certifications.
